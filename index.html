A Heritage Under Siege

Africa is home to some of the most iconic and diverse wildlife on the planet. From the vast savannas of the Serengeti to the dense rainforests of the Congo Basin, these ecosystems rely on a delicate balance maintained by their native species.

However, an unprecedented crisis is unfolding. Over the last 50 years, wildlife populations in Africa have declined by an average of 69%. Driven by poaching, habitat expansion, and climate change, the animals that once roamed freely are now facing the very real threat of extinction.

We believe in an Africa where wildlife and people thrive together. Our science-based, community-centered approach ensures conservation strategies work for both nature and the communities that share these landscapes.

Priority Species We Must Protect

These iconic species are the heartbeat of Africa's ecosystems. Their survival depends on urgent, coordinated action.

Endangered

African Elephant

Loxodonta africana Population: ~416,000

The gentle giants of the savanna are vital ecosystem engineers, shaping landscapes for countless other species. Their populations have plummeted due to the ruthless ivory trade, with an estimated 20,000 elephants killed annually.

Critically Endangered

Rhinoceros

Diceros bicornis Population: ~27,000

Both Black and White rhinos are heavily targeted by poachers for their horns, driven by baseless myths in traditional medicine markets. Three rhinos are killed every day in South Africa alone.

Vulnerable

African Lion

Panthera leo Population: ~23,000

The king of the beasts is losing its kingdom. Habitat fragmentation and human-wildlife conflict have reduced lion populations by 43% in just two decades. They now occupy only 8% of their historical range.

Vulnerable

Cheetah

Acinonyx jubatus Population: ~6,500

The fastest land animal is rapidly running out of space. Requiring vast expanses of land to hunt, cheetahs have lost 91% of their historic range. Only about 6,500 remain in the wild today.

Endangered

African Wild Dog

Lycaon pictus Population: ~6,600

One of the world's most endangered mammals with fewer than 7,000 remaining. Disease, habitat loss, and accidental snaring continually threaten these highly social and intelligent pack hunters.

Endangered

Mountain Gorilla

Gorilla beringei beringei Population: ~1,063

Confined to the misty mountains of Central Africa, mountain gorillas are a conservation success story in progress. Thanks to intensive protection efforts, their numbers have slowly risen from under 300 in the 1980s.

The Major Threats

Understanding the forces driving wildlife decline is the first step toward reversing it.

⚠️ Illegal Poaching

The illegal wildlife trade is a multi-billion dollar criminal enterprise ranking among the top five global illicit activities. Animals are slaughtered relentlessly for their tusks, horns, skins, and bones to supply markets in Asia, Europe, and North America.

🚜 Habitat Destruction

As human populations grow, natural habitats are cleared for agriculture, infrastructure, and settlement. Africa loses approximately 4 million hectares of forest each year, fragmenting ecosystems and isolating wildlife populations.

🌍 Climate Change

Shifting weather patterns cause severe droughts and floods, altering the availability of water and food sources. Africa is warming faster than the global average, devastating already vulnerable ecosystems and species.

🐾 Human-Wildlife Conflict

As human settlements encroach into wildlife territories, conflict intensifies. Farmers lose crops to elephants, livestock to predators, leading to retaliatory killings. Finding peaceful coexistence strategies is critical for both communities and wildlife.
